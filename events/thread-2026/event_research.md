**Event:** Thread, Attentive's own flagship customer conference
**Scope of this doc:** research to inform a specific session/track Attentive runs *at* Thread, not the whole conference
**Status:** Thread 2026 news broke May 26, 2026 ("Attentive Unveils Next Generation of Agentic AI Marketing Innovation at Thread 2026"). ## 1. Who attends

Thread has been consistently **invite-only and brand-side** — no agency, press-only, or general-public tickets. That exclusivity is a deliberate part of the format, not an access limitation.

- **Roles:** Director/Sr. Director/VP-level marketing leaders — specifically CRM, lifecycle/retention marketing, customer communications, and portfolio marketing titles show up repeatedly (e.g., "Sr. Director, CRM & Customer Lifecycle Marketing," "Director, Customer Communications," "VP of Revenue"). This is a practitioner-and-manager audience, not primarily C-suite — closer to the people who own the campaign calendar than the people who own the budget line.
- **Brands:** Overwhelmingly **DTC and fashion/beauty/apparel retail** — Patagonia, SPANX, Mented Cosmetics, CB2, Skechers, Steve Madden, Carter's, Lively, Reformation, YoungLA, Color Wow, Rodial. This tracks Attentive's core ICP closely; it is not an enterprise big-box-retail crowd.
- **Seniority mix:** Practitioner-heavy with a layer of VP sponsorship — i.e., the room is full of the people who will actually configure and run whatever gets pitched, plus enough seniority to approve budget for it.

**Implication for a session:** design for people who already run SMS/email programs day-to-day and will judge content on "can I actually use this," not for an audience that needs the category explained to them.

## 2. What they care about

Cross-referencing named session topics (Section 3) with the 2026 trend research, this audience's live concerns cluster around:

- **Trusting AI enough to hand it real campaign work** — not "should we use AI," but specifically brand-voice control, predictability, and being able to explain what an AI system did and why. Attentive's own Thread 2026 product slate (Brand Voice 2.0, Reporting Agent, Predictive Analytics, AI Campaigns) is a direct response to this exact anxiety.
- **Consolidating tools without losing what worked** — 50%+ of Attentive customers already use multiple Attentive products; the "why unify" conversation is further along with this audience than a cold pitch.
- **BFCM readiness** — Thread 2026's product news was explicitly timed ahead of BFCM 2026; a July/Aug-Sept session sits naturally inside "get ready for Q4" planning cycles for this audience.
- **Proof with real numbers over abstract AI hype** — the audience has already sat through a year of every vendor's "agentic AI" keynote (see Section 4). What's now scarce and valuable to them is a concrete case study with real lift numbers, not another vision talk.

## 4. Competitive landscape

Thread has no on-site sponsor floor of direct competitors the way a third-party conference would — the real competition is for **mindshare**, via what Klaviyo, Bloomreach, Iterable, Braze, and Salesforce say at *their own* flagship customer conferences, since they're pitching the same retail/ecommerce marketing audience.

| Vendor / Event | Scale & format | What they're emphasizing |
|---|---|---|
| **Klaviyo — K:BOS** (Boston, Sept 2025) | 1,000+ attendees, 2 days, 30+ breakouts, celebrity headliners (Paige DeSorbo, Omar Johnson) | "AI-first B2C CRM": launched Marketing Agent (autonomous campaign planning/launch) + Customer Agent (24/7 AI service) |
| **Bloomreach — The Edge Summit** (Miami + London, Sept 2025) | Dual-track: Executive (CMOs/CX heads) vs. Expert (technologists) | "Agentic reality," AI shopping agent "Clarity," privacy-vs-deliverability tradeoffs |
| **Iterable — Activate Summit** (San Jose, Apr 2025) | 3 tracks + virtual encore | Distinct framing: "marketing to both people **and AI agents**," not just using AI internally |
| **Braze — Forge** (Las Vegas, Sept-Oct 2025) | ~600-800 attendees, 100+ speakers (McDonald's, Walmart, Disney, Nestlé Purina) | AI-enabled martech stacks; standout format element: "Braindate" — small-group, informal peer discussions (e.g., "using AI without losing the human touch") |
| **Salesforce — Connections** (Chicago, June 2025) | 13,000+ attendees, 270+ sessions — largest by far | "Marketing is now autonomous" keynote; launched Marketing Cloud Next, reframed marketing as two-way AI-agent-mediated dialogue, not one-way messaging |
| **Yotpo — Destination:D2C / Ecomm Coffee Collective** | Small, casual, community-first | Competes on intimacy, not scale — a genuinely different playbook worth noting |

**The pattern that matters most:** every one of these — Klaviyo, Bloomreach, Iterable, Salesforce, and Attentive itself — is leaning on "agentic AI" as the 2025/2026 headline narrative. **Generic "AI agents are coming" content is now table stakes, not a differentiator.** The sessions that stand out within this set do it through *format* (Braze's peer-intimacy "Braindate," Bloomreach's audience-segmented Exec/Expert tracks) or through a *specific unreplicable angle* (Salesforce's "two-way dialogue" reframe, Iterable's "marketing to AI agents" angle) — not through louder AI claims.

## 5. Best-practice formats/benchmarks

- **Timing norms:** breakouts ~50-60 min with a buffer before the next session; workshops ~75-90 min with a mid-session break; fireside chats tighter, ~30-40 min, exactly one guest (multiple guests drifts into an unstructured panel).
- **Format-to-purpose fit:**
  - *Case studies* — best for credible proof; audiences specifically value hearing what didn't work, not just the win.
  - *Panels* — only earn their slot when panelists genuinely disagree or bring distinct vantage points; otherwise they read as a string of monologues (a real risk given how homogenous the "AI agents" narrative already is across vendors — see Section 4).
  - *Fireside chats* — best when the value is one candid, high-signal voice, not breadth.
  - *Workshops* — attendees leave having produced something concrete (a drafted brief, a mapped journey); most engagement-dense format but needs tighter attendee caps and dedicated facilitators.
- **What drives engagement:** a mix of formats beats repeating one; live polls/structured Q&A lift even passive formats; small-group peer formats (Braze's Braindate is the closest direct comp) are repeatedly cited as more memorable than lecture/panel defaults.
- **Common mistakes to avoid:** building content without a clear target-attendee profile in mind; letting the agenda page go stale during the promotion window (prospective attendees check it repeatedly before committing); treating the live session as a standalone moment instead of part of an always-on before/after campaign; generic mass post-event follow-up; measuring booth-traffic/swag instead of qualified engagement.

## The wedge for standing out

Every competitor above is already telling this exact audience "AI agents are coming" — that message is fully saturated going into Thread 2026. A session that leads with generic agentic-AI hype will read as a weaker echo of what Klaviyo, Salesforce, Braze, Bloomreach, and Iterable each already said at their own 2025 flagships.

Attentive's genuine, hard-to-copy wedge is the **combination**, not either half alone: Attentive is the one platform in this set that is **mobile/SMS-first at the identity-graph level** ("conversational commerce" has been Thread's own framing since 2022) *and* now has a real agentic layer sitting on top of that identity graph (AI Campaigns, Reporting Agent, Predictive Analytics, Brand Voice 2.0, all unveiled for Thread 2026). A Klaviyo, Braze, or Salesforce speaker cannot credibly claim the mobile-first half — none of them originated around SMS the way Attentive did. The session should be built around **"agentic AI operating inside a consent-based, mobile-identity-first channel"** — proven with one real brand's numbers — rather than another abstract "AI agents in marketing" talk. That framing is Attentive's alone to make.
